Four years ago, Ashoka Rajendra started **Orchestra Bio** to fix a slow, messy part of medicine: running a drug-development programme. Now the startup has been acquired by **OpenAI**.

## What Orchestra Bio does

Making a new medicine is a long journey. It usually takes 10 to 15 years, with thousands of decisions across research, lab studies, manufacturing and clinical trials. Orchestra Bio builds AI tools that help pharma and biotech companies keep track of these programmes. Its software follows studies, keeps teams on the same page and flags delays before they hurt.

## Why OpenAI wants it

OpenAI has been building **Rosalind**, its set of AI models and tools for life-sciences research. Orchestra Bio's team and technology will now be joined with Rosalind. The idea is to link scientific discovery with the practical work that follows: planning experiments, running studies and deciding the next steps for a possible medicine.

OpenAI already works with big drug makers such as **Novo Nordisk** and **Moderna**. Orchestra Bio gives it a product built for the operations side of drug development.

## What happens to the founder

Rajendra has joined **OpenAI's Life Sciences team** after the deal. The terms of the acquisition have not been disclosed.

## Why it matters

For founders, this is a clear signal. Big AI companies are now buying focused startups that solve one hard industry problem very well. A small team that knows its sector deeply can become a valuable piece of a much bigger platform.
