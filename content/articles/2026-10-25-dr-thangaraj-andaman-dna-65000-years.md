Some of the oldest chapters of human history are written in the DNA of a few tribes in the forests of the **Andaman Islands**. The scientist who read them is **Dr Kumarasamy Thangaraj**, a geneticist at **CSIR-CCMB (Centre for Cellular and Molecular Biology), Hyderabad**.

## The Andaman discovery

In **2005**, Dr Thangaraj and his colleagues published a study in the journal **Science** on the DNA of Andaman tribes, the **Onge** and the **Great Andamanese**.

Their finding: these tribes descend from the **earliest humans to leave Africa**. Those first migrants moved along the coast, by the **southern coastal route**, and reached the region **about 65,000 years ago**.

## Two ancient populations behind most Indians

In **2009**, a study in the journal **Nature** by David Reich, Dr Thangaraj and colleagues showed that **most Indians today descend from two ancient populations**: the **Ancestral North Indians (ANI)** and the **Ancestral South Indians (ASI)**.

![Graphic of the ANI and ASI streams merging into India](/images/dr-thangaraj-andaman-dna-65000-years-ani-asi.jpg)

## Three decades of work, and a Padma Shri

Dr Thangaraj has spent **more than three decades at CCMB**. In **2026**, the Government of India honoured him with the **Padma Shri** in the field of **Science and Engineering**.

His work is a reminder that history is not only in books and ruins. Some of it is in our own DNA, waiting for someone patient enough to read it.
