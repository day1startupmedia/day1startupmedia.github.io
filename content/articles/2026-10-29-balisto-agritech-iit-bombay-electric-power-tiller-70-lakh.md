Two IIT Bombay graduates left big corporate jobs to fix a very ground-level problem: Indian farmers are short of labour and spend more and more on diesel. Their Pune-based startup, **Balisto Agritech**, now makes electric farm machines and has reported **₹70 lakh in revenue in about 15 months**.

## From Harvard, UPSC and Swiggy to the farm

**Aishwarya Ramakrishnan** and **Kedar Gokhale** are from IIT Bombay's **2010 batch**. After IIT, Aishwarya did an MBA at **Harvard Business School** and worked in **Mahindra Group's farm equipment business**. She also co-founded the education NGO **Avanti Fellows**.

Kedar **cleared the UPSC exam twice**, but did not get the cadre he wanted and went back to the corporate world. He rose to **Vice President (Operations) at Swiggy**.

Driven by an interest in climate and agriculture, the two old classmates teamed up in **February 2023**. The company was registered in **February 2024** as Higher Orbit Agritech Pvt Ltd (the brand was earlier called Orbit AgriTech), and later raised **pre-seed funding from angel investors**.

## The machine: Balisto 130 Pro

After meeting local farmers, the founders saw three big problems: shortage of labour, physical strain from traditional tools, and the rising cost of diesel and expensive machines.

Their answer is the **Balisto 130 Pro**, a **13 HP electric multipurpose power tiller**. It handles ploughing, weeding and earthing-up in crops like **sugarcane, cotton, paddy, turmeric and ginger**, with an adjustable working width of **2 to 5 feet**.

- Two detachable **4.6 kWh LFP lithium-ion batteries**
- A smart control unit that adjusts speed and torque to the soil
- Low vibration and an emergency safety lever for the operator
- **IoT sensors** that send machine data to a server for remote monitoring and servicing

## Dealers, not D2C

Instead of selling directly online, Balisto chose a **dealer-led model** so farmers get local after-sales service. It has **15 dealers in Maharashtra**, and also reaches hundreds of farmers through **rental agri-entrepreneurs** who rent the machines out.

Next, the founders plan remote-controlled machines, attachments such as sprayers and trolleys, and a **battery-as-a-service** model.
