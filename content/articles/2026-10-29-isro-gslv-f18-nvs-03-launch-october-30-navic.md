India's space agency **ISRO** is set to launch **NVS-03**, its next navigation satellite, on the **GSLV-F18** rocket on **Friday, 30 October 2026**, from the **Second Launch Pad at Satish Dhawan Space Centre, Sriharikota**.

## What ISRO has confirmed

According to ISRO's official mission page:

- **GSLV-F18** is the **20th flight** of India's Geosynchronous Satellite Launch Vehicle (GSLV).
- It will place **NVS-03** into a **Geosynchronous Transfer Orbit**.
- NVS-03 is the **third satellite** in India's next-generation navigation series, built to keep **NavIC** (IRNSS) navigation services running.
- The satellite carries an **indigenous (made-in-India) atomic clock**, along with procured atomic clocks.
- It adds the **L1 signal**, alongside the existing L5 and S-band signals, with coverage over India and about **1,500 km around India's borders**.

## Launch date and time

Livemint, Moneycontrol, The Indian Express and BusinessLine all report the launch for **30 October (Friday)**. Some reports and social posts give a late-night launch time of around **11:51 PM IST**, but ISRO's mission page does not list the exact time, so check ISRO's live stream for the final time. ISRO has a live streaming page for the mission.

## Why it matters

NavIC is India's own satellite navigation system, like a desi GPS. Reports from India Today and The Times of India note that NavIC has had gaps since the earlier NVS-02 satellite ran into trouble, and NVS-03 is an important step to strengthen it. ISRO is said to have made changes to its approach for this mission based on lessons from NVS-02.

## Fact check

A viral post about this launch is **mostly correct**: the rocket (GSLV-F18), the satellite (NVS-03), the place (Sriharikota) and the date (30 October) all match ISRO and major news reports. The exact time is the only detail ISRO has not confirmed on its own page.
